\begin{abstract}
Papers that make vision-language-action (VLA) policies cheaper to run
justify their speedups by showing no loss on LIBERO, where base policies
typically succeed in more than $90\%$ of episodes. We show that such
no-loss verdicts are largely supplied by tasks both settings already
solve. With binary outcomes two settings can disagree on at most the
episodes where one of them fails, so saturated tasks force agreement and
tighten an absolute-difference interval without informing it about the
tasks where policies fail. On released $\pi_{0.5}$, one denoising step
is equivalent to ten within $\pm5$ points on an eight-task battery but
inconclusive on the tasks with headroom; of $\NumCensusEquiv$
certificates among $\NumCensusPairs$ paired comparisons, saturated tasks
keep $\NumCensusSatKept$ and informative tasks $\NumCensusEquivKept$ at
equal episode counts. Where failures are common, one step costs $\pi_0$
$\NumPiZeroCost$ points and $\pi_{0.5}$ $\NumCamHardCost$ points on the
hardest camera-viewpoint shifts. Among $\NumEffInc$ VLA efficiency papers
claiming parity on LIBERO, the median base policy succeeds in
$\NumEffMedianBase\%$ of episodes, where a $\pm5$-point margin tolerates
$\NumEffAllowedRatio\times$ the base's failures, and $\NumEffAny$ report
any uncertainty, per-task result, or paired analysis. We give a
three-part report for no-loss claims and a tool that computes it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Making VLA policies cheaper at inference is now a research area of its
own: fewer denoising steps~\cite{snapflow2026,chen2026simple},
caching~\cite{oi2026actioncache}, adaptive computation~\cite{han2026adavla},
token pruning, and quantization, applied to released policies such as
$\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, and SmolVLA~\cite{black2024pi0,pi05,shukor2025smolvla}.
Their shared evidence is parity with the base policy on
LIBERO~\cite{liu2023libero}; released $\pi_{0.5}$, for example, loses
$0.2$ points at a single denoising step~\cite{oi2026actioncache}.\footnote{\ifanon Code, per-episode logs, and audit extractions are provided as anonymized supplementary material.\else Per-episode logs, audit extractions, and code: \url{https://github.com/nihalgunu/roborigor}\fi}

\textbf{Claim.} Such no-loss verdicts are largely supplied by tasks both
settings already solve, and so say little about the tasks on which
policies fail. The underlying leniency of absolute margins at low event
rates is known in non-inferiority trials~\cite{quartagno2020,redfors2024},
and in language-model compression aggregate parity is known to hide
per-example changes~\cite{dutta2024accuracy,hooker2019forget,kubler2026degradations},
with a recent audit of equivalence claims for compressed
LLMs~\cite{singh2026certifying}. We isolate what those works do not:
on a saturated benchmark, disagreement is bounded by failures, so the
certificate is decided by the few tasks that still fail, and it does not
transfer to them. \textbf{Contributions:} (1)~a quantification of this
dilution for VLA evaluation, with an equal-size control over task
subsets (Sec.~\ref{sec:dilution}); (2)~evidence of where the shortcut
costs something once failures are common (Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer});
(3)~an audit of $\NumEffInc$ VLA efficiency papers showing their claims
sit in this regime, and a three-part report with a tool that computes it
(Secs.~\ref{sec:audit},~\ref{sec:report}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
In robot learning, the LBM study compares policies with paired initial
conditions and posterior uncertainty~\cite{lbm2026}, and sequential
tests stop comparisons early~\cite{snyder2025step}. On
LIBERO specifically, most state-of-the-art claims cannot be significant
given their headline scores~\cite{jiang2026benchmarking}, undocumented
evaluation details move scores by tens of points~\cite{choi2026vlaeval},
and perturbed variants expose large drops that clean scores
hide~\cite{fei2025liberoplus,zhou2025liberopro}. These works concern
claims that one policy beats another, or robustness; the no-loss claims
that efficiency methods rest on, and how saturation certifies them,
have not been examined for robot policies.

\section{Setup}\label{sec:protocol}
Released $\pi_{0.5}$ and $\pi_0$ LIBERO checkpoints (openpi) and SmolVLA,
served through one harness; $31{,}858$ per-episode records are released.
The battery is eight LIBERO-10 tasks $\times$ 20 initial states $\times$
3 sampling seeds ($n{=}480$ per setting). A design written before any
grid episode classified four tasks as informative (development-scan
success in $[0.5, 0.95]$: tasks 0, 2, 8, 9) and four as saturated, for a
different study; using it here is our choice after the fact. Under ten
steps the informative tasks succeed in $95$, $97$, $80$, and $95\%$ of
episodes and the saturated ones in $97$--$100\%$, so the split rests
chiefly on task 8. Shifts are fixed LIBERO-Plus sets~\cite{fei2025liberoplus}:
$312$ object-layout variants and $419$ camera-viewpoint variants (two
draws each), each with benchmark-defined difficulty levels 1--5. ``One
step'' is a single Euler step of the released flow head, against ten,
both at execution horizon 10. Comparisons are paired on (initial state,
seed) or (variant, draw) and tested with exact McNemar~\cite{mcnemar1947};
intervals are Newcombe's paired score intervals~\cite{newcombe1998paired}.
A comparison is a significant difference if $p{<}0.05$, otherwise
equivalent if its 95\% interval lies within $\pm5$ points, otherwise inconclusive.

\section{Saturated Tasks Supply the Certificate}\label{sec:dilution}
\textbf{One contrast.} On the battery, $\pi_{0.5}$ succeeds in $0.965$ of
episodes at one step and $0.946$ at ten ($23$--$14$ discordant pairs,
$p{=}0.19$): equivalent, $[\NumBatteryLo, +\NumBatteryHi]$. Admitting tasks
from the hardest to the easiest, the interval fits inside the margin
only at the end (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}a). On the four informative
tasks it is $[\NumHeadroomLo, +\NumHeadroomHi]$, inconclusive; nothing
here shows a cost, but nothing certifies its absence either.

\textbf{Every contrast.} The released grids hold $\NumCensusPairs$ paired
comparisons between dial settings on identical episodes ($\pi_{0.5}$ at
two environment seeds, and $\pi_0$). On the battery $\NumCensusEquiv$
certify equivalence (all on $\pi_{0.5}$, over $\NumCertCells$ distinct
settings, so not independent) and $\NumCensusDiff$ differ ($\NumHolmDiff$
after Holm). At the same $240$ pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}b), the
four saturated tasks keep $\NumCensusSatKept$ of the $\NumCensusEquiv$
certificates but only $\NumDiffKeptSat$ of the $\NumCensusDiff$
differences; the four informative tasks keep $\NumCensusEquivKept$ and
$\NumCensusDiffKept$. The informative half is the unique minimum of all
$70$ subsets, and subsets without task 8 keep a median of
$\NumCensusNoEight$ certificates against $\NumCensusWithEight$ with it.
The asymmetry holds under unpaired Newcombe ($\NumUnpSat$ vs.\
$\NumUnpInf$), paired Wald ($\NumWaldSat$ vs.\ $\NumWaldInf$), a 90\%
rule ($\NumNinetySat$ vs.\ $\NumNinetyInf$), and a $\pm7$ margin
($\NumSevenSat$ vs.\ $\NumSevenInf$).

\textbf{Why.} For binary outcomes the probability that two settings
disagree on an episode is at most the sum of their failure rates,
$f_A{+}f_B$; on the battery that bound is $\NumDiscBound\%$ and the
observed disagreement $\NumDiscObs\%$. Saturated tasks therefore
contribute pairs that cannot disagree: they leave the discordant counts
$b, c$ unchanged while the difference $(b{-}c)/n$ and its standard error
shrink as $1/n$, faster than the $1/\sqrt{n}$ of informative pairs.
Equivalently, the pooled difference is the task-weighted mean of
per-task differences: if a share $w$ of pairs comes from tasks that can
fail, a pooled certificate at $\pm m$ is compatible with an average loss
of up to $m/w$ on those tasks. Task 8 carries $\NumTaskEightShare\%$ of
the battery's ten-step failures but one eighth of its pairs, so a $\pm5$
battery certificate is compatible with a $40$-point loss on it.
Certifying $\pm5$ on the informative tasks directly would take about
$\NumCertifyPairs$ paired episodes against the $240$ the battery allots.

\section{Beyond the Saturated Battery}\label{sec:transfer}
On released $\pi_0$, whose battery rate is lower, one step costs
$\NumPiZeroCost$ points ($0.773$ vs.\ $0.852$, $33$--$71$,
$p{=}\NumPiZeroP$, $[\NumPiZeroLo, \NumPiZeroHi]$): whether one step
suffices depends on the policy. SmolVLA is inconclusive
($[\NumSmolLo, +\NumSmolHi]$). Under object-layout shift, where $\pi_{0.5}$
still succeeds in $91\%$ of episodes, one step is equivalent
($[\NumLayoutLo, +\NumLayoutHi]$). Under camera-viewpoint shift it costs
$\NumCameraCost$ points overall ($45$--$84$, $p{=}0.0008$,
$[\NumCameraLo, \NumCameraHi]$), and the cost sits where the benchmark
places its hardest shifts: at levels 4--5 one step loses
$\NumCamHardCost$ points ($\NumCamHardA$--$\NumCamHardB$,
$[\NumCamHardLo, \NumCamHardHi]$), while at levels 1--3 the contrast is
inconclusive ($[\NumCamEasyLo, +\NumCamEasyHi]$). ActionCache likewise
reports $\pi_{0.5}$ collapsing at one step on the harder VLABench~\cite{oi2026actioncache}.

\section{The Literature's No-Loss Claims}\label{sec:audit}

papers with numeric LIBERO rates. Shaded: base at or above $90\%$. Blue:

Under a written search and extraction protocol ($\NumEffScreened$
candidates, two-stage screening), $\NumEffInc$ VLA inference-efficiency
papers claim parity or acceptable loss against their own base policy on
LIBERO, spanning step reduction, distillation, caching, token pruning,
quantization, early exit, and speculative decoding
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency}). The base succeeds in at least $90\%$ of
episodes in $\NumEffCeilCount$ of the $\NumEffPlotted$ claims with
numeric rates (median $\NumEffMedianBase\%$). At that median a $\pm5$-point
margin tolerates $\NumEffAllowedRatio\times$ the base's failures, and in
$\NumEffDoubleK$ of $\NumEffDoubleN$ claims it tolerates at least double.
Only $\NumEffAny$ of $\NumEffInc$ report any uncertainty, per-task result,
or paired analysis, and $\NumEffNoN\%$ state no usable episode count. The
same comparisons do not lose more on the harder benchmarks the papers
also report: the loss is larger there in $\NumEffLarger$ of
$\NumEffPairs$ papers and smaller in $\NumEffSmaller$ (sign test
$p{=}\NumEffSignP$), though those benchmarks are author-selected and
often small real-robot tests where lower latency itself helps. The audit
thus shows that the field's certificates are uninformative, not that the
certified shortcuts are costly. Extraction was LLM-assisted; text search
of a seeded $20\%$ sample confirmed $\NumEffVerAuto$ of
$\NumEffVerFields$ fields, and manual checks the rest.

\section{A Report for No-Loss Claims}\label{sec:report}
Relative margins remove the dilution but fail the other way: the
failure-rate ratio ignores solved tasks, yet under camera shift, where
most episodes fail, it is $\NumFrCam$ $[\NumFrCamLo, \NumFrCamHi]$ and
would certify non-inferiority at a $1.5$ bound although failures rise
significantly. We therefore recommend reporting (i)~the discordant pairs
with the exact conditional odds-ratio interval; (ii)~the absolute
interval on tasks declared informative before the experiment; and
(iii)~a failure-rate-ratio interval against a declared bound, certifying
only if (ii) lies inside the margin and (iii) lies below the bound.
\texttt{\ifanon ceilingcheck\else roborigor\fi{} report} computes all three from paired records and
warns when a pooled certificate does not hold on the informative tasks. Table~\ref{tab:report} applies the report: the battery certificate
fails (ii) because its informative tasks are inconclusive, and the two
costs are flagged by all three parts.

\textbf{For authors.} A no-loss claim that the report can certify is
cheap to design: pair every episode by initial state and seed; pick the
informative tasks from a pilot scan before the comparison, keeping those
with room to fail; size that subset for the disagreement rate the pilot
shows (about $\NumCertifyPairs$ pairs at the $\NumHeadroomDisc\%$ we
observe); and state the margin and ratio bound before running.

\textbf{Limitations.} Simulation only, on LIBERO-family benchmarks and
three flow-matching policies. The certificates in our grids come from
one policy and share arms, the informative/saturated split rests chiefly
on one task and was made for a different study, and we re-test one
acceleration; the audit shows other families make the same kind of
claim but does not re-test them.

\textbf{Conclusion.} A no-loss verdict on a saturated benchmark is valid
for the average it certifies and uninformative about the tasks on which
policies fail. The VLA efficiency literature makes its claims in exactly
this regime; they should come with discordant pairs, results on
informative tasks, and a relative margin.
