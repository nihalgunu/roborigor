\title{Three Dials of Flow-Policy Inference:\\What Actually Moves VLA Success Rates}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{\ifanon Anonymous\else Nihal Gunukula\fi}}

\begin{abstract}
Flow-matching vision-language-action policies ship with three inference
dials that the field sets by folklore: the number of denoising steps
(typically 10), the execution horizon before replanning (typically 5),
and the sampling seed (typically unreported). We characterize all three
on LIBERO under a significance-first protocol with exact intervals and
paired designs. Two dials have sharp Pareto structure: for $\pi_{0.5}$,
a \emph{single} Euler integration step executed over a horizon of 10 is
statistically indistinguishable from the 10-step default at $3\times$
lower control latency, its one measured price a
confirmed $\NumCameraCost$-point robustness cost under
camera-viewpoint shift; replanning every step costs
$\NumEveryStepLo$--$\NumEveryStepHi$ points across all three
policies we test. The third dial is not a dial at
all: rerunning the standard 500-episode protocol with a different
flow-sampling seed spans $\NumSeedSpread$ points, a band that
already contains $\NumSeedShare\%$ of the recomputable improvements
highlighted across 50 recent VLA papers, and grows to
$\NumBatterySpread$ points on informative mid-difficulty tasks. A nested
decomposition shows flow-sampling noise, not the initial state,
dominates outcome variance wherever the benchmark is informative.
Across four LIBERO-Plus perturbation axes, pairwise margins moved
wildly (a $\NumCleanGap$-point clean gap stood for anything from
$\NumGapLayout$ to $\NumGapMax$ points of deployed margin) while
policy \emph{rankings}
inverted only where an axis drove the trailing policies toward the
floor: for policies separated as ours are, ordering is stable off the
floor; margins never are. All claims ship
with pre-registered analyses, a public deviations log, per-episode
logs, and a pip-installable harness with an exact rollout-budget
calculator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Flow-matching action heads are now the dominant recipe for
vision-language-action (VLA) policies, a line advancing
rapidly~\cite{black2024pi0,pi05,shukor2025smolvla,pi07}, and their
inference behavior is
governed by three dials every deployment sets implicitly: the number of
ODE integration steps, the number of actions executed before
replanning, and the random seed feeding the flow sampler. Published
evaluations almost never vary, report, or cost these dials. We measure
what each one buys, what each one costs, and what each one does to the
statistical meaning of a reported success rate
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}).\footnote{Per-episode logs for every reported number, the frozen pre-registration with its deviations log, the audit extraction, and the harness \ifanon are prepared for anonymous release; the link is withheld here only to preserve anonymity and appears in the camera-ready.\else are released at \url{https://github.com/nihalgunu/roborigor}.\fi}

on $\pi_{0.5}$: inference dials the evaluator sets (dark) and

(Secs.~\ref{sec:pareto}--\ref{sec:margins}).}
\label{fig:hero}

The cost of folklore is measurable. Auditing 50 recent VLA papers
under frozen intake rules, over a third of their highlighted
comparisons carry no usable rollout count; at the audited median of 50
rollouts per task (IQR 20--50), the minimum detectable effect at
LIBERO-typical base rates is $\NumMdeFifty$ points; nearly a third of
the recomputable highlighted comparisons fail an exact test at their
own rollout counts, and nearly half fall below their own protocol's
MDE~(Sec.~\ref{sec:audit}). The dials
and the statistics are not separate problems: two dials move the
number, and the third alone spans more than a quarter of the field's
claimed advances.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~A $4{\times}3$ Pareto map of denoising steps~$\times$~execution
horizon with exact confidence intervals (Sec.~\ref{sec:pareto});
(2)~a nested variance decomposition separating flow-sampling noise
from initial-state variation, with system nondeterminism measured and
bounded (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}); (3)~a significance-first evaluation protocol,
budget calculator, and released per-episode logs; (4)~a perturbation study
across four LIBERO-Plus axes and three policies: one $16.5$-point
clean gap stands for $\NumGapLayout$ to $\NumGapMax$ points of
deployed margin, and rankings invert only in the floor regime
(Sec.~\ref{sec:margins}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Inference dials as improvement levers.} Recent work treats
denoising steps and initial noise as levers for making flow policies
better or faster~\cite{snapflow2026}, and real-time chunking
\cite{black2025rtc} redesigns execution scheduling for reactive
deployment. We measure the same dials as \emph{evaluation variables}:
what they do to the reported number and its uncertainty. Our
single-step result needs no distillation or retraining, holds on
the unmodified public checkpoint, and is policy-conditional, which is
a different claim from making few-step policies \emph{trainable}. Our horizon
finding is the benchmark-side complement of RTC, not a contradiction of
it (Sec.~\ref{sec:pareto}).

\textbf{Evaluation methodology.} Kress-Gazit et
al.~\cite{kressgazit2024empirical} set out best practices; Jiang et
al.~\cite{jiang2026benchmarking} audit LIBERO significance and find
most SOTA claims unprovable; we extend that audit with per-comparison
minimum detectable effects, unit-tagged rollout counts, and
non-reporting as a first-class outcome. STEP~\cite{snyder2025step} and
its successor~\cite{snyder2026beyond} provide anytime-valid policy
comparison; our paired e-process and the design-effect correction from
measured variance components build directly on them.
PhAIL~\cite{phail2026} ships a distributional real-robot protocol;
we are simulation-scale and success-rate-focused. Active experiment
selection~\cite{anwar2025active} is complementary to our budget
calculator.

\textbf{Perturbation suites.} LIBERO-Plus~\cite{fei2025liberoplus}
supplies the perturbation battery; we evaluate frozen variant sets
over four of its seven axes (camera viewpoint, object layout,
lighting, robot initial state);
LIBERO-PRO~\cite{zhou2025liberopro} adds semantics-breaking probes
whose floor effects are degenerate for rank analysis, so we cite rather
than run it.

\section{Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Every reported aggregate is regenerated from per-episode JSONL
records by released scripts, and a released consistency check pins
the paper's numerals, tables, and generated macros to those records;
no aggregate is carried by hand.
\textbf{Design.} LIBERO exposes deterministic initial states indexed
per task, so all comparisons are paired episode-for-episode; per-task
comparisons use the exact McNemar test on discordant pairs, suite-level
families use Holm correction, exploratory per-task families use
Benjamini--Hochberg~\cite{holm1979,bh1995}. Intervals are
Clopper--Pearson~\cite{clopper1934} (Wilson~\cite{wilson1927} intervals ship
alongside in the artifact summaries; conclusions are
interval-choice-invariant), differences use
Newcombe's score interval~\cite{newcombe1998} (computed unpaired,
which is conservative under the positive correlation that pairing
induces), unpaired literature
recomputations use Boschloo's exact test~\cite{boschloo1970} below
the registered sample-size cap and Fisher's exact test above it
(deviations log, Entry 1).
\textbf{Seed semantics.} A ``sampling seed'' names a noise
\emph{stream}, not a draw: each action chunk receives a seed derived
from (episode seed, chunk index), so a replicate reproduces the whole
trajectory's noise sequence and consecutive chunks never reuse noise.
Fixed-request determinism is bitwise on our A100 reference; closed-loop
replicates at fully fixed factors still disagree on 7.6\% of cells,
which we report as system-level nondeterminism and fold into the
decomposition rather than assume away.
\textbf{Equivalence margin.} The $\pm5$-point margin used
throughout was fixed at the design reframe, anchored to the audit's median per-comparison MDE ($4.35$ points), an
external quantity: it postdates the
$4{\times}3$ grid itself but predates the distribution-shift
(Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion}), replication, and camera data (deviations
log, Entry 5(a)). The headline
equivalence verdict is not margin-fragile: its Newcombe interval
$[-1.2, +4.1]$ sits inside any margin down to $\pm4.2$ points.
\textbf{Pre-registration.} Analysis choices, intake rules for the
literature audit, and go/no-go thresholds were frozen before data
collection; the released deviations log records every post-freeze
change, including the direction of this paper after two registered
provocations failed (Sec.~\ref{sec:negatives}).

\section{Dials One and Two: Steps and Horizon}\label{sec:pareto}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_pareto.pdf}
\caption{Success rate vs.\ control latency for all 12
$(\textit{num\_steps}, \textit{exec\_horizon})$ cells ($n{=}480$ each,
Clopper--Pearson 95\% intervals; latency from the measured A100 cost
model). For $\pi_{0.5}$ the folklore default $(10,5)$ is dominated;
the frontier is a single integration step at horizon 10
(Sec.~\ref{sec:replication} for what replicates on $\pi_0$).}
\label{fig:pareto}
\end{figure}
We evaluate $\pi_{0.5}$ on a $4{\times}3$ grid of denoising steps
$s\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ and execution horizon $h\in\{1,5,10\}$ over the
registered eight-task LIBERO-10 battery (20 paired initial states, 3
sampling seeds; $n{=}480$ per cell; 5{,}760 episodes, exactly-once
coverage verified against the campaign manifest;
Table~\ref{tab:grid}). The battery is the
V1 selection rule applied to prior per-task rates: the four tasks then
inside the informative band $p\in[0.5,0.95]$ plus the four
least-saturated others; task 5 (saturated at $1.00$) was excluded by
that rule, and task 7 was dropped from a three-way $0.98$ tie with
tasks 1 and 3 by a lowest-index tie-break.

\textbf{The default is dominated.} The published default $(s{=}10,
h{=}5)$ reaches $0.950$ at $16.2$\,ms of amortized control latency per
executed step (latency modeled from measured per-component costs, not
per-cell wall clock). The frontier cell $(s{=}1,h{=}10)$ reaches $0.965$ $[0.944,0.979]$
at $5.6$\,ms (Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}); nothing on the grid dominates
it, and per-chunk inference cost falls from $81$ to $56$\,ms. Held to
this paper's own standard, the right claim is equivalence, not
superiority: paired by initial state against the default, the exact
McNemar test does not separate them ($18$ vs $11$ discordant wins,
$p{=}0.26$) and the Newcombe interval on the difference,
$[-1.2, +4.1]$ points, establishes equivalence within $\pm5$ points.
A tenth of the denoising budget and a third of the latency buy a
statistically indistinguishable success rate. Mechanistically this is
expected: one Euler step from the prior executes approximately the
conditional-mean action chunk, and the measured flow-sampling variance
at $(1,10)$ is $0.023$ versus $0.052$ at $(10,10)$, consistent with
near-deterministic mean-chunk execution on a benchmark where the mean
action suffices. Intervals in Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto} are
Clopper--Pearson over episodes; init-cluster bootstrap intervals are
wider in $11$ of $12$ cells (median $+36\%$, up to $+54\%$; the
saturated $(10,10)$ cell is $7\%$ narrower) and change no conclusion
(artifact).

\textbf{The dials interact.} At $h{=}1$ (replan every step), steps
matter: success climbs from $0.642$ to $0.848$ as $s$ goes $1\to10$.
At $h{=}10$ the effect inverts: $s{=}1$ is nominally best. Replanning
cadence, not denoising budget, is the dominant dial: every $h{=}1$ cell
loses $10$--$32$ points to its $h{=}10$ counterpart. The measured
variance components couple the same way: the flow-sampling variance component rises steeply as steps fall at
$h{=}1$ ($0.058\to 0.117$) and flattens at $h{=}10$, i.e., frequent replanning repeatedly
re-exposes the trajectory to sampler noise.

\textbf{Scope.} These results are conditional on LIBERO's quasi-static
manipulation: nothing in the scene demands mid-chunk reactivity. On
tasks that do, tight closed-loop execution is the point, and real-time
chunking~\cite{black2025rtc} addresses precisely the latency--reactivity
tension that our open-loop frontier sidesteps. We state the finding as
benchmark-conditional; its value is that the benchmark in question is
the field's primary one.

\subsection{Does the cheap frontier survive distribution shift?}
\label{sec:fusion}
The natural objection to a mean-chunk shortcut is that its slack
appears only off-distribution. We re-ran $s\in\{1,2,10\}$ at $h{=}10$
on identical LIBERO-Plus variant sets ($688$ variants, one paired
episode per variant per cell). Under
object-layout perturbation the cheap cells hold exactly: $0.920$ vs
$0.913$ for $s{=}1$ vs $s{=}10$. Under camera-viewpoint perturbation
the cost is real, by the strongest evidence in this paper: a
pre-declared confirmatory sample (all $419$ camera variants of the
fork suite, two fresh draws per variant at each step count, $838$
pairs, single registered analysis; deviations log, Entries 7 and 9)
measures $0.406$ vs $0.452$, a $4.7$-point deficit, discordants
$45$--$84$, exact McNemar $p{=}0.0008$ (unpaired Newcombe interval
$[-9.4, +0.1]$ points, conservative under pairing). The exploratory
draws that motivated it ($632$ pairs, pooled $p{=}0.017$; deviations
log, Entries 4(d), 5(b)) also carried this paper's central caution in
miniature: an equivalence screen that pooled camera with layout read
an ambiguous $p{=}0.076$ where the camera-only paired test was
already $p{=}0.015$; the wrong aggregation, like the wrong rollout
count, manufactures apparent equivalence. A second step does not
recover the cost: $s{=}2$ patterns with $s{=}1$ under camera
perturbation ($0.468$ vs $0.505$) while holding fully under layout
($0.907$ vs $0.913$), so the deficit is a many-steps effect, not a
one-versus-two effect. The honest summary: the cheap-steps frontier
is free on-distribution and under layout shift, and carries a
confirmed $4.7$-point robustness cost under viewpoint shift, a price
we attach to the deployment recommendation rather than bury.

\subsection{Which parts replicate on a second policy?}
\label{sec:replication}
Rerunning the key cells on $\pi_0$ (same battery, paired inits,
$n{=}480$ per cell) separates the universal from the policy-specific.
The horizon effect replicates emphatically: replanning every step
costs $\pi_0$ $40.8$ points at $s{=}1$ ($0.365$ vs $0.773$) and
$18.1$ points at $s{=}10$ ($0.671$ vs $0.852$). The single-step cell
is again not separated from the $(10,5)$ default ($0.773$ vs $0.787$,
McNemar $p{=}0.56$; difference CI $[-6.7, +3.8]$ points, consistent
with equivalence but too wide to establish it at $\pm5$), but
unlike $\pi_{0.5}$, $\pi_0$ pays a real price for
dropping steps at $h{=}10$: $(10,10)$ beats $(1,10)$ by $3.0$--$12.8$
points (paired $p{=}0.00025$). The scoped conclusion: execution
horizon dominates denoising budget for both policies, while
single-step sufficiency is a property of the policy, not of the
benchmark, which sharpens the mechanism reading, since whether the
conditional-mean chunk suffices is exactly what should vary across
training recipes. Finally, at a second environment seed $\pi_{0.5}$'s
single-step cell reproduces to within one episode in $480$
($0.9625$ vs $0.9646$)
and never trails the default there either (paired difference
$[+0.1,+5.9]$ points in the frontier's favor, $p{=}0.02$): the
headline cell is stable across policies' shared protocol, environment
seeds, and, per Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion}, layout perturbation.
A third policy widens the pattern: SmolVLA~\cite{shukor2025smolvla},
a compact non-openpi flow policy served through the same harness,
shows the same horizon ordering on an eight-task battery ($h{=}10$
$0.648$ vs.\ $h{=}5$ $0.596$, paired discordants $63$--$38$, McNemar
$p{=}0.017$, $n{=}480$ per cell) and pays a real every-step price:
$0.554$ at $h{=}1$ ($9.4$ points below $h{=}10$; paired difference
CI $[3.2, 15.5]$, $p{<}10^{-4}$). That number carries its own
cautionary tale: an interim look at this cell, truncated at $26$
episodes that happened to all come from the battery's hardest task,
read $2/26$ and briefly suggested outright collapse; the pre-declared
completion to the designed $n{=}480$ (deviations log, Entries 4(c),
6, 8) shows a task-composition artifact, not a collapse. Partial
looks at non-random subsets manufacture effects; the full cell is
the measurement. Its
single-step cell is statistically indistinguishable from its default
($0.625$ vs.\ $0.648$, paired difference $-2.3$ points
$[-8.3, +3.8]$, $p{=}0.29$), though the interval is too wide to claim
equivalence at our $\pm5$-point margin. Horizon dominance is thus
three-for-three across two model families while its magnitude is
policy-dependent ($10$--$32$ points for $\pi_{0.5}$, $18$--$41$
for $\pi_0$, $9$ for SmolVLA), and single-step sufficiency holds
for two of the three policies, with $\pi_0$ the exception that
pays.

\section{Dial Three Is Not a Dial: the Seed}\label{sec:seed}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_seed_spread.pdf}
\caption{Ten runs of the standard 500-episode LIBERO-10 protocol
differing only in flow-sampling seed (5{,}000 fresh episodes). The
2.2-point spread contains 27\% of the 86 recomputable improvements
highlighted across the 50 audited papers.}
\label{fig:seed}
\end{figure}
Fixing everything but the seed exposes the third dial for what it is,
at two scales. At the field's standard protocol (the full LIBERO-10
suite, 50 initial states per task, 5{,}000 fresh episodes across ten
flow-sampling seeds) the headline rate spans $2.2$ points (SD $0.64$,
mean pairwise shift $0.71$; Fig.~\ref{fig:seed}). That band is not
small relative to what the field reports: $27\%$ of the $86$
recomputable highlighted improvements are $\le 2.2$ points ($34\%$
when restricted to LIBERO-family comparisons, where the band was
measured), and $19\%$ sit within two seed-noise standard
deviations. The band itself is
measured on one policy and one suite; policies and suites with lower
base rates will have wider ones (Sec.~\ref{sec:pareto}'s variance
coupling). The band also grows with
benchmark informativeness: on a battery weighted toward
mid-difficulty tasks (the V1 grid marginalized by seed, $160$
episodes per replicate), the same reseeding spans $7.5$ points (SD
$2.4$), the scale of the field's median claimed gap ($7.2$). Part of
that growth is mechanical (the smaller replicate implies
$\sqrt{500/160}\approx1.8\times$ the SD); the measured
$3.8\times$ exceeds it, so roughly a $2\times$ factor is
informativeness itself.
Saturated protocols do not remove seed sensitivity; they hide it in
tasks whose outcome no longer varies.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_varstacks.pdf}
\caption{Outcome-variance components per battery task: initial-state
share (dark) vs.\ within-init flow-sampling variance (light).
Saturated tasks (grey, $\mu\ge0.96$) contribute almost nothing to
either component.}
\label{fig:varstacks}
\end{figure}
\textbf{Where the variance lives.} With initial states fixed and seeds
varied ($8$ tasks $\times$ up to $40$ inits $\times$ $10$ seeds), a
nested beta-binomial decomposition attributes outcome variance to the
initial state (ICC) versus flow-sampling noise, with a
method-of-moments cross-check and cluster bootstrap over inits
(Fig.~\ref{fig:varstacks}). On the
informative task (base rate $0.59$), the flow-sampling variance
component is $0.204$ of a $0.242$ total ($84\%$ of outcome variance;
init ICC $0.16$ $[0.06, 0.24]$): at a fixed initial state, the
sampler's noise stream largely decides the outcome. On near-saturated tasks ($\mu \ge 0.96$)
every component degenerates: five of the eight battery tasks sit at
or above $0.96$ in our own measurement despite four qualifying at
selection time, the quantitative form of the observation that the benchmark can
no longer separate frontier policies.
In our own 20-init scan of the other three LIBERO suites, no task
falls below $0.90$, and every task not at ceiling scores exactly
$18/20$ or $19/20$. At $n{=}20$ this certifies no task as clearly
informative while it cannot exclude mildly informative ones (an
$18/20$ interval reaches down to $0.68$); by our own budget table,
settling that census would take hundreds of episodes per task.
The flow-sampling component as estimated includes residual system
nondeterminism; the measured $7.6\%$ replicate disagreement bounds that
contribution above by $0.038$ of variance, well below the $0.204$
measured on the informative task.
\textbf{The decomposition replicates across environment seeds.}
Rerunning the four qualifying tasks at two further environment seeds
($24$ inits $\times$ $10$ sampling seeds each, $1{,}920$ fresh
episodes) reproduces the structure: on the informative task the
sampling component is $0.200$ and $0.214$ (vs.\ $0.204$), the
init-dominated task keeps its high init share ($0.57$ at both new
seeds), and the near-saturated tasks stay degenerate. The
\emph{level}, by contrast, moves: the informative task's base rate
shifts from $0.59$ to $0.53$ and $0.54$. Where the variance lives is a
stable property of the policy--benchmark pair; the headline number is
not.
\textbf{And across policies.} The same instrument cell rerun on
$\pi_0$ and SmolVLA ($40$ inits $\times$ $10$ sampling seeds each)
gives the same verdict at very different competence levels: with the
initial state fixed, flow-sampling noise accounts for $84\%$,
$81\%$, and $61\%$ of outcome variance for $\pi_{0.5}$
(base rate $0.59$), $\pi_0$ ($0.16$), and SmolVLA ($0.30$)
respectively, with the method-of-moments cross-check agreeing in every
case. Averaging over sampling seeds is not an optional refinement for
some particular policy; it is where most of the per-init variance
lives across the flow-policy class.

\textbf{What this costs.} At the field's default of 50 rollouts per
task, the minimum detectable effect at base rate $0.95$ is $18.3$
points ($80\%$ power, exactly enumerated); $500$ episodes buy $4.6$ points and
$1{,}500$ buy $2.5$. Our released calculator inverts these exactly for
paired and unpaired designs, and the measured components supply the
design-effect correction for sequential tests~\cite{snyder2025step}.

\section{Rankings Survive Off the Floor; Margins Do Not}\label{sec:margins}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_perturb.pdf}
\caption{Clean vs.\ perturbed success for three policies on identical
LIBERO-Plus variant sets. Lines never cross (ordering stable) while
separations change drastically.}
\label{fig:perturb}
\end{figure}
\textbf{Rankings survive perturbation; magnitudes do not.} Across three
policies ($\pi_{0.5}$, $\pi_0$, SmolVLA) evaluated on an identical set
of 688 LIBERO-Plus variants (Fig.~\ref{fig:perturb}), not one pairwise ordering inverted at the
axis level (Kendall $\tau{=}1.0$; a null simulation that redraws
observed rates under binomial noise with the clean ordering preserved
yields $p{=}1.0$; the single nominal level-scan inversion has
$p{=}0.26$). Clean anchors come from our own paired 200-episode runs
on the same suite ($\pi_{0.5}$ $0.960$, $\pi_0$ $0.795$, SmolVLA
$0.645$), not from published numbers; the ordering conclusion is
unchanged under either choice. Meanwhile the \emph{gaps} moved wildly
in both directions: the $16.5$-point $\pi_{0.5}$--$\pi_0$ gap nearly
tripled to $45.7$ under camera perturbation and fell to $9.6$ under
layout; the $15.0$-point $\pi_0$--SmolVLA gap collapsed to $4.5$ under
camera and more than tripled to $53.8$ under layout. Two caveats
scope this result: the no-inversion analysis here covers two of the
seven LIBERO-Plus axes (the two further axes below extend coverage to
four, and find the floor-regime exception), and
with perturbed separations this large the design has little power to
detect inversions between closely matched policies; the informative
finding is the magnitude instability, not the absence of flips. The
practical reading is good news with an asterisk: the field's
leaderboard ordering is more trustworthy than our premise assumed, but
a clean-benchmark gap carries almost no information about the
robustness margin behind it.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_newaxes.pdf}
\caption{Success by perturbation level on two further axes (frozen
variant sets, identical across all three policies). Lighting degrades
without reordering; robot-initial-state shift floors $\pi_0$, where
the clean $\pi_0$--SmolVLA ordering inverts ($p{=}0.0022$).}
\label{fig:newaxes}
\end{figure}
\textbf{Two further axes find the regime where ordering fails.}
On frozen variant sets for two additional axes run on all three
policies ($252$ lighting and $370$ robot-state variants,
Fig.~\ref{fig:newaxes}), the axes behave qualitatively
differently. Under Light Conditions every clean ordering is preserved
emphatically ($\pi_{0.5}$ $0.921$, $\pi_0$ $0.794$, SmolVLA
$0.238$; $\pi_0$--SmolVLA discordants $146$--$6$). Under Robot
Initial States the top of the order holds: $\pi_{0.5}$ at $0.730$
degrades gradually from $0.92$ to $0.45$ while $\pi_0$ is floored at
every level ($0.059$, discordant pairs $250$--$2$, a $67.0$-point
gap). At the floor, however, the clean ordering breaks: SmolVLA ($0.122$)
significantly outscores $\pi_0$ ($38$--$15$ discordants, exact
$p{=}0.0022$, unchanged under Holm across the six pairwise tests of
this section; exploratory, as this axis postdates the freeze),
inverting a $15$-point clean-benchmark gap. The
collapse is not a serving artifact: $\pi_0$ scores $0.794$ under
Light Conditions through the identical fork wrapper, server process,
and session (matching its clean $0.795$), and $\pi_{0.5}$ handles the
same robot-state variants through the same code path, so the
observation pipeline and the perturbation application are both
exonerated; what remains is $\pi_0$'s own brittleness to shifted
initial joint states. The
pattern is a two-regime rule. While every policy retains headroom,
perturbation rescales gaps without reordering (the same
$16.5$-point clean gap stands for anything from $9.6$ to $67.0$
points); once
an axis floors the competitors, even the ordering carries no
information.

\section{The Pre-Registered Gates and the Audit}\label{sec:negatives}\label{sec:audit}
This paper began as a provocation with two pre-registered go
conditions, and both failed. We report them because their failure is
informative and because a pre-registration that only survives success
is theater. The first condition required rank inversions under
perturbation at the registered scale; on the two registered axes the
ordering held everywhere while pairwise gaps across all three policy
pairs ranged from $4.5$ to $63.5$ points around clean gaps of $15.0$
to $31.5$, and the gate was called as failed. A post-gate extension to two
further axes (deviations log, Entry 4(b)) later found one significant
inversion in the floor regime (Sec.~\ref{sec:margins}), consistent
with the registered condition's spirit but outside its frozen scope,
so we report it as exploratory. The second required at least one third of
audited comparisons to fail exact tests at their stated rollout
counts, or the audited median MDE to exceed the median claimed gap;
the measured rate fell just short ($32.6\%$) and the median MDE
($4.35$) sits below the median gap ($7.2$), so both disjuncts failed.

\textbf{The audit, reduced to what survived.} Across 50 papers sampled
under frozen intake rules (stratified 2024--2026, newest-first,
no discretionary skips), $32.6\%$ of the 86 recomputable highlighted
comparisons fail an exact test at the papers' own rollout counts: real,
but under our registered one-third threshold, and the median highlighted
gap ($7.2$ points) exceeds the median per-comparison MDE ($4.35$). The
statistic that needs no threshold: $34.4\%$ of highlighted comparisons
carry no usable rollout count at all ($45/131$: not reported or
unit-ambiguous), $13$ of $50$ papers $[15\%, 40\%]$ report no usable
count on any extracted comparison (two further papers publish success
rates only as figures), and eval seed counts appear for only $31$ of
$131$ comparisons. Eleven comparisons pit the authors' method at known $n$
against baseline rates copied from other papers at unknown $n$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:audit}). The
full extraction, intake logs, and deviations log ship with the

\textbf{Report the dials.} Steps, horizon, and seed policy move the
number by up to $\NumEveryStepHi$ points across the three policies
we test (Sec.~\ref{sec:pareto}); a success rate without them is not a
measurement. \textbf{Size the run before launching it.}
Table~\ref{tab:power} is the cost of honesty at LIBERO-typical base
rates: the field's median protocol (50 rollouts) resolves only
$18$-point gaps, and a $2$-point claim costs $2{,}200$ episodes per
arm. \textbf{Pair everything.} LIBERO's indexed initial states make
episode-level pairing free; on our own key comparisons the
measured relative efficiency of the paired design over the unpaired
one is $1.4$--$2.3\times$ (per-comparison values in the artifact). \textbf{Average over sampling seeds} on any task
inside the informative band, where the sampler carries $61$--$84\%$
of per-init outcome variance (Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}); report the seed
count. \textbf{Report a margin band, not a clean gap}: a clean gap
predicts deployed separation only up to a factor of seven
(Sec.~\ref{sec:margins}). \textbf{Stop early honestly} with
anytime-valid sequential tests~\cite{snyder2025step,snyder2026beyond}
corrected by the measured design effect.

The harness (\ifanon released; name withheld during
review\else\texttt{pip install roborigor}\fi) runs every policy as a
websocket server speaking the openpi client protocol, so a new policy
is one serve script of roughly a hundred lines; episodes land as append-only JSONL
records whose factor-complete key gives exactly-once resume, cross-box
integrity checks, and the pairing used throughout this paper. Noise
is seeded per (episode, chunk) so replicates are bit-reproducible
where the stack allows, and the residual $7.6\%$ system
nondeterminism is measured rather than assumed away
(Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}). The paper's numerals, tables, and the
abstract's generated macros regenerate from the released records by
the released scripts.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
All results are simulation-only and LIBERO-family; the horizon
finding is conditional on quasi-static tasks (Sec.~\ref{sec:pareto}).
Initial states remain the paired factor throughout;
environment-seed variation is measured separately
(Sec.~\ref{sec:seed}). SmolVLA illustrates a
final caution: its default-configuration per-task success spans
$0.25$ to $0.98$ across the battery ($n{=}60$ each), so suite-pooled
numbers can hide task-level competence differences that matter for
deployment.
